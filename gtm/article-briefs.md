# Cornerstone article briefs — AI Spirit

Four neutral, category-agnostic articles for the first 30 days. Each one is built to:
- Rank for a head-of-funnel keyword
- Internally link to ~10 `/talk-to/[slug]` pages (SEO equity)
- End with a single, clean CTA: "Start chatting → /personas"

**Cadence:** ship one per week for the first four weeks. Target ~1,500–2,000 words. Original screenshots from the product where possible (Google rewards real product imagery).

---

## Article 1 — "Best AI chatbots in 2026: a no-fluff comparison"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| URL | `/blog/best-ai-chatbots-2026` |
| Primary keyword | `best AI chatbot 2026` |
| Secondary keywords | `AI chatbot online`, `chat with AI for free`, `Character AI alternatives` |
| Search intent | Commercial investigation — user evaluating options |
| Word count | 1,800–2,200 |
| Internal links | 10–12 to `/talk-to/[slug]` pages spanning multiple categories |

**Outline:**
1. **Hook (100 words)** — One short story: "I asked five AI chatbots the same question — here's what happened." Sets up an honest comparison, not a listicle disguised as one.
2. **The shortlist (300 words)** — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Character.AI, Replika, AI Spirit. One-line positioning for each.
3. **What "best" actually means (300 words)** — Frame the evaluation: persona depth, conversational memory, voice, free tier, privacy. Set up criteria your reader can carry into any tool.
4. **The five chatbots, scored (700 words)** — Honest comparison table. AI Spirit wins on "personas with depth" and "free tier without paywall pressure." Don't oversell; readers see through it.
5. **The category you actually want (300 words)** — "If you want X, use Y." Slot AI Spirit into the persona-conversations slot. Link out to 6–8 personas across categories: a spiritual guide, a historical figure, a fictional character, a thinker, a companion. This is where the SEO equity flows.
6. **Quick CTA (100 words)** — "Try a conversation now — no signup needed to browse." Single button to `/personas`.

**Voice rules:** No "X+ personas." No "Indian." No superlatives like "the most powerful." Honest, slightly dry, comparative.

## Article 2 — "Talk to historical figures with AI: what we built, what worked, what didn't"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| URL | `/blog/talk-to-historical-figures-ai` |
| Primary keyword | `talk to historical figures AI` |
| Secondary keywords | `chat with Einstein AI`, `AI famous people`, `historical figures chatbot` |
| Search intent | Informational + product-discovery |
| Word count | 1,500–1,800 |
| Internal links | 8–10 historical persona pages (`/talk-to/einstein`, `/talk-to/gandhi`, etc.) |

**Outline:**
1. **Hook (150 words)** — Open with a real conversation excerpt: a question, then the AI persona's answer. Make it surprising.
2. **Why people search for this (200 words)** — Briefly: curiosity, learning, creative writing, just-for-fun. Don't be condescending — readers know why they want it.
3. **What separates a good historical persona from a bad one (400 words)** — Source material density, era-appropriate language, intellectual honesty about limits ("I cannot speak to events after my death"), willingness to disagree with the user. Show examples — good vs bad responses side by side.
4. **The personas we built and what surprised us (500 words)** — Short profiles of 6–8 historical personas with one example exchange each. Link each name to its `/talk-to/[slug]` page.
5. **Limits and caveats (200 words)** — Be honest: these are not the real people, not infallible, sometimes wrong. This earns trust and reduces backlash from skeptical readers.
6. **CTA (100 words)** — "Pick a name from history → start the conversation." Button to `/personas?category=Historical`.

**Voice rules:** Show, don't sell. Real example exchanges beat any marketing copy. Don't claim "the most accurate" — say "we worked hardest on X."

## Article 3 — "AI personas for self-reflection: a guide to using AI for journaling and clarity"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| URL | `/blog/ai-personas-for-self-reflection` |
| Primary keyword | `AI for journaling` |
| Secondary keywords | `AI for self-reflection`, `AI life coach`, `AI for clarity`, `talk to AI about feelings` |
| Search intent | Informational + emotional |
| Word count | 1,500–1,800 |
| Internal links | 6–8 personas spanning spiritual + companion + intellectual categories |

**Outline:**
1. **Hook (150 words)** — One paragraph: a real user's anonymized story. Stuck on a decision. Talked to an AI persona. Got unstuck. Note: clarity, not therapy.
2. **What AI is good (and bad) at for self-reflection (300 words)** — Good: rubber-ducking, structuring jumbled thoughts, asking the question you've been avoiding. Bad: replacing a therapist, diagnosing, giving medical advice. Be clear-eyed.
3. **Five prompts that actually work (500 words)** — Concrete prompts users can copy. Examples: "I'm avoiding deciding between X and Y. Ask me 5 questions to figure out what I actually want." Each prompt followed by a sample response.
4. **Which persona for which purpose (400 words)** — Spiritual guide for life direction. Stoic philosopher for resilience. Wise friend for emotional honesty. Internal links to specific personas. Two sentences each, no more.
5. **The boundaries to set (200 words)** — When to log off. When to call a human. Direct line to a crisis resource (iCall India: 9152987821, or equivalent).
6. **CTA (100 words)** — "Try a 5-minute session — pick a persona that fits your mood." Button to `/personas`.

**Voice rules:** Earnest, not preachy. Honest about limits. This article builds trust more than it converts; that's the point.

## Article 4 — "Why we built AI personas (and what we learned shipping the first 100 conversations)"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| URL | `/blog/why-we-built-ai-personas` |
| Primary keyword | `AI personas` |
| Secondary keywords | `how AI chatbots work`, `building AI characters`, `AI character creator` |
| Search intent | Informational + brand-building |
| Word count | 1,800–2,200 |
| Internal links | 8–12 across categories + 1 link to `/personas` (the index) |

**Outline:**
1. **Hook (200 words)** — Founder POV. One paragraph on why this matters. Don't fake humility, don't oversell. "We wanted to make AI feel like a person you'd want to talk to, not a tool you have to operate."
2. **The decision tree behind a persona (500 words)** — What goes into a good system prompt. Tone, knowledge limits, conversation starters, refusal patterns. Walk through one persona end-to-end. Make it educational, not gated content.
3. **Three things users do that surprised us (500 words)** — Three real (anonymized) usage patterns: late-night journaling, role-played job interviews, talking to "future self." Each anchored in a specific persona link.
4. **What we got wrong in v1 (300 words)** — Personas that broke character, retention drop-off after first chat, the day we launched without the share button. Vulnerability earns links and shares.
5. **What's next (200 words)** — Tease without promising dates. Memory across personas, voice mode, custom personas users can build themselves.
6. **CTA (100 words)** — "Start a conversation that goes somewhere. Pick a persona →" Single button to `/personas`.

**Voice rules:** First-person, founder voice. Specifics over abstractions. This is the article that gets shared on Twitter/LinkedIn and earns inbound links — write it last, when you have real numbers and stories.

## Distribution after publish (per article, ~30 min)

1. Post on **LinkedIn** (founder voice, lead with the most interesting paragraph).
2. Post on **Twitter/X** as a 4–6 tweet thread; pin to profile for a week.
3. Submit to **Hacker News** (Article 1 and 4 only — these are the linkable ones).
4. Submit to **r/InternetIsBeautiful**, **r/Sideproject** (Article 4 only).
5. Cross-post excerpt on **Indie Hackers** (Article 4 only).
6. Add to the **footer** of every `/talk-to/[slug]` page under a "From the blog" section once 3+ articles exist.

## Tracking

For each article, track in GA4:
- Organic sessions to the article URL
- Click-through rate from article → `/personas` and `/talk-to/[slug]` pages
- Signups attributed to article-originated sessions (set up via UTM-tagged internal links)

Review at 30 days. Any article in bottom 50% — rewrite the title and intro, don't replace the article (Google rewards refreshed content, not deletion).
